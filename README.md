# CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective for the SNHU Travel project. Throughout this course, I explored the Product Owner, Scrum Master, developer, and tester roles and learned how Agile and Scrum support software development.

## Project Artifact

The Sprint Review and Retrospective document reflects on my experiences during the course, including writing user stories, developing test cases, implementing features, and responding to changing requirements.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by identifying what users want to accomplish and why it matters to them. User stories help turn those needs into smaller, understandable features by describing the user, the desired action, and its purpose. In the SNHU Travel project, the top five destinations user story gave me a clear goal for development. Acceptance criteria and test cases help me check whether the finished feature meets that goal. When requirements are unclear, I would ask questions instead of making assumptions about what the user wants.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by breaking a larger problem into smaller tasks, building one part at a time, and testing as I go. This makes problems easier to identify and gives me room to adjust when requirements change. In future projects, I hope to continue using user stories, a prioritized backlog, and short development cycles. I also want to use regular feedback and retrospectives to improve both the program and how I work. This course helped me understand the value of completing manageable pieces of working software and adapting based on what I learn.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, following through on commitments, and being willing to give and receive feedback. It also means asking for help when needed and sharing problems early so they do not delay other people’s work. During this course, taking on different Scrum roles helped me understand how developers, testers, and the Product Owner depend on one another. A good teammate considers those different perspectives, supports others, and stays focused on the team’s shared goals.
